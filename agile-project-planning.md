# Agile Project Planning Report

## 1. Introduction
This mini-project applies Agile planning practices to the proposed Student Record and Academic Management Platform. The goal is to organize requirements into epics and user stories, prioritize delivery, estimate effort, and plan iterative development.

## 2. Product scope
Core scope: authentication, student management, course management, attendance, marks/results, and basic reporting. Notifications and advanced analytics are planned as later capabilities if time permits.

## 3. Stakeholders
- Students
- Faculty members
- Academic office staff
- System administrators
- Institutional decision-makers

## 4. Assumptions and constraints
- Stakeholders will clarify policies and approve requirements.
- The team has limited academic-project time.
- The system must protect student data.
- Story-point estimates are relative and will be revised as the team learns more.

## 5. Agile approach
Use Scrum-style planning with a Product Backlog, Sprint Goals, Sprint Backlogs, reviews, and retrospectives. A proposed sprint length is two weeks; the team may adjust it to the course schedule.

## 6. Prioritization approach
Prioritize by business value, user importance, dependencies, risk, and complexity. Authentication and core student records come before advanced analytics.

## 7. Estimation approach
Use Planning Poker and a relative Fibonacci-like scale (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13). The numbers represent relative effort and uncertainty, not hours.

## 8. Risk register
| Risk | Impact | Proposed response |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements are unclear | Rework and delays | Confirm user stories and acceptance criteria with stakeholders |
| Student data is exposed | High | Use role-based access, secure sessions, and minimum necessary access |
| Scope grows too quickly | Sprint goals may fail | Keep MVP small and defer lower-priority features |
| Estimates are inaccurate | Unreliable sprint plan | Re-estimate after learning and track actual capacity |
| Grading rules are not defined | Incorrect results | Obtain approved grading rules before implementing calculations |

## 9. Definition of Done
A story is Done when its acceptance criteria are met, relevant checks pass, documentation is updated, and the team has reviewed the work.

## 10. Expected deliverables
Product vision, Scrum role structure, epic and story documentation, acceptance criteria, prioritized backlog, estimates, sprint plan, Scrum board, burndown chart, ceremony reports, framework comparison, and release roadmap.

## 11. Conclusion
Incremental delivery and regular stakeholder feedback can reduce risk and make the platform easier to validate. The plan should be updated based on real team capacity and review feedback.
